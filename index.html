Limitations + Variations = Program
Entwurf und Anwendung eines Schriftsystems
Design and application of a type system

How do systematic restrictions influence the legibility and form of letters, and ultimately the entire typeface?

As part of this project, a programmatic approach was developed in which a single grid system was populated with letterforms created from variable modules. Five basic typeface styles, each forming an aesthetic unit, were derived from the same four modular components and systematically applied to the entire alphabet. The goal was to design a coherent type system in five different versions, all sharing a visual identity based on the same structural foundation.

What makes this system unique is the modularity and variability of its components: even changing a single module can fundamentally alter the visual appearance of all letters across the five versions. This results in numerous new typefaces emerging from one defined structure. A multiplying, dynamic process.

Exhibited:
Call Me #8 – Jupiter Hamburg (2025)
Rundgang Finkenau – HAW Hamburg (2024)

Furthermore, the modules were designed to be interpolable. This means they can transition smoothly between two or more states within a group. As a result, seamless transitions and novel intermediate forms emerge along an axis. This principle allows type to be understood as a flexible, transformable system that unfolds within the tension between rule and variation.

The variations developed within this program clearly demonstrate that systematic restrictions do not solely impose limitations, but also unlock creative potential. They influence legibility, the formal expression of the letters, and ultimately shape the overall appearance of the typeface.

How not to Work (HN2W)
Commandments of Procrastination

This (kinetic) poster series questions the widespread consensus around a work ethic focused on self-optimization. Its starting point is How To Work Better, a 1991 piece by the Zurich-based artist duo Peter Fischli and David Weiss, which outlines ten rules for efficient work, inspired by factory instructions from Thailand. Prominently displayed on building façades in Zurich and Manhattan, this set of rules resembles the Ten Commandments in form, casting the employer as a godlike authority while the artists act as mediators.

The rules reflect an ideology of productivity enhancement, the promises of which often go unfulfilled in reality. As an ironic counterpoint, the series picks up the theme of procrastination and stages it typographically. Common procrastination strategies echo the subversive spirit of Fischli and Weiss, exposing the original rule set as a corporate ideology wrapped in idealistic language.

The «Commandments for Distracted and Inefficient Work» offer a humorous yet realistic alternative to this ideal. They provide a glimpse into work processes that are far removed from disciplined self-optimization. A perspective aimed at portraying the working world for people with attention difficulties or ADHD. From this emerges the thesis that procrastination can also be understood as a conscious or unconscious form of resistance to the logic of exploitation and optimization.

How not to Work (HN2W)
Ignore the Problem

The three-part poster series visually interprets the progression of a work process influenced by concentration difficulties and procrastinative behavior. The sequence follows a familiar pattern: initial avoidance, growing inner restlessness, and ultimately the overwhelmed attempt to tackle all tasks at once. The numerical order of the posters is deliberately rearranged to emphasize the chaotic nature of the process.

Step #1: «IGNORE THE PROBLEM» — The work process begins with avoidance. Tasks are ignored, and attention shifts to everything except what actually needs to be done.

Just do everything all at once

New Design from Düsseldorf
Complete Visual Identity for a Design Exhibition

Working in a team of six people over the course of two semesters, we developed the entire visual identity for the third edition of the design exhibition New Design from Düsseldorf at the NRW-Forum Düsseldorf.

The formal and aesthetic exploration of fluidity, in combination with the sustainable materials used throughout the exhibition, creates a distinctive visual language within the exhibition space and also forms the abstract conceptual foundation of the graphic design: "New Design – New Materials."

The exhibition graphics include, but are not limited to, a 360-page catalog published by Slanted Publishers, exhibition posters, flyers, animations for various screens, flags, banners, merchandise, and wall graphics.

Seen On Screen
On alcohol consumption and product placement
in Hollywood movies
_Archive Magazine
_210 mm × 297 mm (DIN A4)
_152 pages
_Cover
_Index Insert (Gatefold)
_Softcover

As part of this magazine project, a curated collection of film scenes from Hollywood productions was compiled to highlight the increasing presence of product placement in relation to alcohol consumption, specifically through the brands Heineken and Budweiser. A custom-created film archive documents these placements chronologically, making it possible to trace the growing frequency and normalization of alcohol-related brand placements over the decades.

The magazine is supplemented by a separate insert: a four-page index lists all registered product placements, including their frequency. Films in which alcohol brands are explicitly advertised or implicitly promoted are highlighted in red — illustrating the close connection between consumer culture and cinema.

Critical
Typeface
Black, Black Italic

Critical is a bold grotesque typeface designed in 2023. It is characterized by a robust and stable structure and includes an italic, dynamic alternative style. The design is inspired by other heavy black typefaces commonly used in digital applications. Critical was created for use in contexts that require typography that is striking, distinctive, and at the same time highly legible.

Highwayman
Typeface

Highwayman is a typeface developed in early 2024. Its design is inspired by classic serif fonts found in ancient Roman inscriptions and 19th-century American typography, interpreting them in a contemporary form. Characteristic of the typeface are the pronounced, long, and sharp serifs, which give the uppercase letters a stable and powerful appearance. Highwayman is particularly suited for use in headlines, posters, or other areas where a bold yet tradition-conscious typographic style is desired.

In addition, Highwayman is also suitable for body text. The lowercase letters are designed to ensure good readability, even at smaller font sizes.
